What Carolina has to do against the No. 3 Irish

North Carolina is 2-1. Notre Dame is 4-0 and has outscored opponents 169-33. It is the first meeting since 2022, when the Irish won 45-32, and Notre Dame leads the series 21-2. UNC has to play close to perfectly, and the numbers show why.

The Irish are excellent at avoiding the situations where a defence can dictate play. If they sit in second-and-six or third-and-two, the Tar Heel pass rush has little to work with. If UNC pushes them into third-and-seven or third-and-eight, the matchup changes.

UNC has to make Notre Dame play behind the chains. That is the path Carolina has.

This is the biggest statistical gap on the field. Notre Dame's passing EPA per play is +0.570 (11th nationally) against +0.148 (94th) for UNC. In FBS-only games, Carr is at 9.59 yards per dropback and Edwards at 5.34.

At Clemson, Edwards went 13 of 30 for 135 yards. The run game carried the offence, but once Clemson loaded the box and forced obvious passing situations, drives stalled. That is exactly where Notre Dame wants this game to go. Some blame lies with his receivers, but Edwards also needed to plant his feet and steady himself before many throws.

His legs are a real asset. FBS-only tracking credits him with 61 rushing yards on seven carries (8.71 per carry). A few boots, scrambles and pocket movements can turn a third-and-eight into a third-and-three, which is very different from asking him to throw 40 times.

UNC cannot turn Billy into Carr for an afternoon. It has to make his job easier.

Run game Strength vs strength

UNC running attack

Benjamin Hall: 215 yards on 33 carries (6.5 per carry), 130 on 18 at Clemson.
Line: 4.02 line yards per carry (2nd) and a 58% run success rate (5th).

Notre Dame run defence

28% rushing success rate allowed, -0.229 rushing EPA per play, 2.33 line yards per carry, and a 17% havoc rate (5th nationally).

Abandoning the run would be a mistake, and so would running into Notre Dame's strength on every early down. UNC should stay committed to Hall while using formations, motion and misdirection to stop the Irish stacking the interior. If he consistently gets four to six yards, forces respect for play-action and keeps UNC out of third-and-long, he has done his job.

The concern is fumbling: Hall fumbled against TCU and Demon June fumbled at Clemson. On the other side, Notre Dame's backs (Aneyas Williams and Nolan James Jr., 4.3 yards per carry each) are less scary than the passing game, but a 53.1% rushing success rate (ninth) means they keep the Irish ahead of schedule.

The individual matchup: Xavier Lewis

Lewis has six sacks and 8.5 tackles for loss through three games, and he can win from the interior and from wide alignments. Notre Dame has protected Carr well, with left tackle Will Black the reported soft spot, so UNC should move Lewis around and use stunts rather than let him face the same double team all afternoon. One tackle for loss on first down or one second-down sack can change how a drive develops.

Carolina cannot let Edwards drop back on second-and-11 or third-and-10. Notre Dame would happily take that matchup and run away with it.

Receivers Irish edge

Notre Dame is where Carolina's secondary has its biggest problem. Micah Gilbert has 334 yards and two touchdowns on 15 catches, and Jordan Faison has 239 yards and two touchdowns. Gilbert's FBS-only line is 13 catches for 251 yards, with an 80% success rate. UNC cannot sell out against one receiver, and Jaden Greathouse, Jerome Bettis Jr. and the tight ends give Carr more places to go.

UNC's secondary allows 6.0 yards per pass attempt, but it looked horrendous at Clemson. It does not need to shut down Carr; it needs enough negative plays and incompletions to end drives, and Greg Smith already has two interceptions.

North Carolina is less proven outside Jordan Shipp and Jelani Thurman. Thurman leads with 183 yards, but Shipp has been far more productive per play in FBS games: 11 catches on 15 targets for 139 yards at +0.85 EPA per play, against 7 catches on 17 targets at -0.22 for Thurman. Shipp is the steady possession receiver, while Thurman is a receiving tight end who should be used in motion and in space. Suspended freshman CJ Sadler is a possibility to play, though I expect he won't.

Get Shipp involved early. UNC's offensive explosiveness ranks 135th, so the passing game needs reliable completions, not hero throws.

That gives Carolina a clearer job than surviving 70 snaps: make Notre Dame drive the length of the field. Carr is too good to assume he will make mistakes on his own, so the aim is to make him work for everything. Mix pre-snap looks, disguise blitzes and simulated pressure, and do not play scared and give up easy underneath throws.

The linebackers matter here. Derek McDonald (24 tackles) and Peyton Seelmann (20) cannot chase backs five yards downfield; they have to diagnose fast and get downhill. Notre Dame's secondary is deep, so UNC should create separation before the catch with motion, crossers, play-action and quick outs, rather than throw contested deep balls.

Where the game turns

  1. Win early downsForce second-and-long and third-and-seven or longer, so Lewis and the front can get to Carr.
  2. Stay balanced on offenceKeep leaning on Hall, June and Edwards' legs, and mix in high-percentage throws. Third-down conversion is 38.2% for UNC against 41.5% for Notre Dame, and UNC allows 44.4% to the Irish's 35.2%.
  3. Protect the footballNotre Dame is +2.25 in turnover margin per game to UNC's +0.50, and has already scored a defensive touchdown. No short fields.
  4. Finish drivesAt Clemson UNC settled for a field goal inside the seven. Kicker Aeron Burrell is 4-for-7 on field goals, so touchdowns matter. Notre Dame commits just 2.5 penalties per game, so UNC has to take every point.

What to watch early

Bad signs

Notre Dame runs for five or six yards a carry, Carr is comfortable on first down, Hall gets hit behind the line, or Edwards keeps facing third-and-eight to third-and-10.

Good signs

UNC runs efficiently, Edwards completes easy throws, and the Irish face third-and-seven or longer.

The verdict

Notre Dame is the more complete team: the more efficient quarterback, the more dangerous passing game, the stronger defence and far more margin for error. But UNC is not empty-handed. It has a real running game, a defensive line that can create havoc, and a defence far more consistent than its offence.

Clemson showed where the margin for error is: Carolina cannot wait three quarters for the offence to find a rhythm. If it gets everything working for four quarters, the run game, the defence and the pass rush give it something to build on. That is what Saturday at Kenan Stadium will test.
